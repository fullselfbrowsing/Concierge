- use-after-stop Error message: `This session has stopped.`
- construction Error message: `The session could not start.`
- empty catalog: one frozen readonly array reused for no-context and best-effort stop clearing.

Diagnostic code/message table is exact:
- catalog_publish_failed: `The transport rejected a catalog publication, so the session was stopped.`
- batch_dispatch_failed: `The dispatcher could not complete an accepted batch; later batches will continue.`
- response_failed: `The transport rejected a result; it was not retried.`
- stage_listener_failed: `A stage subscriber threw; remaining subscribers will continue.`
- transport_subscribe_failed: `The transport could not register a session subscription; construction was rolled back.`
- transport_unsubscribe_failed: `The transport could not remove a session subscription; remaining cleanup continued.`
- catalog_clear_failed: `The transport could not clear its catalog; remaining cleanup continued.`
- abort_signal_failed: `A batch cancellation signal failed; the batch was treated as cancelled.`
- batch_without_context: `A batch arrived before session context was set and was ignored.`
</interfaces>

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Session.stop → transport/application callbacks | Cleanup callbacks can throw or reenter while authority and work are being invalidated. |
| Stage listener → setContext/stop | Consumer callbacks can mutate listener/state order during notification. |
| Caught runtime failure → diagnostic sink | Caught data and raw identifiers may contain secrets, and the sink itself may throw. |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Severity | Category | Component | Disposition | Mitigation and verification |
|-----------|----------|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------------------|
| T-07-04 | high | Tampering / Denial of service | stop drain, transition cleanup, subscribers | mitigate | Cache/mark and invalidate transition generations plus the publication token before outside code, detach/abort/drain active/queued/published-but-unconfirmed accepted records, use tokenized snapshots, continue cleanup independently, and suppress post-stop output; C13/C14 and L01-L13 are blocking. |
| T-07-05 | high | Information disclosure / Denial of service | diagnostics and hostile callbacks | mitigate | Closed fresh frozen messages, no-binding catches, replacement semantics, secret-absence tests, and continuation under hook/console throws; L12/L14-L16 are blocking. |
| T-07-01 | high | Elevation of privilege | post-stop stale authority | mitigate | Stop clears queued/in-progress publication state and invalidates generation/token before outside code; no late successful return may update published/confirmed state, subscriptions become inert, and empty catalog is attempted afterwards; C13/C14 and L02/L05/L08/L13 are blocking. |
| T-07-02 | high | Repudiation | detached accepted work | mitigate | Every detached queued, publication-in-progress, or published-but-unconfirmed occurrence dispatches exactly once but emits zero post-stop responses; C13/C14 and L03/L04 count both facts independently. |
| T-07-SC | low | Tampering | package supply chain | accept | No package is installed; the implementation uses factory-local maps/promises and existing host/signal seams. |

## Residual Risk

A transport unsubscriber that throws may retain an inert callback, and an entered handler may ignore cancellation and delay drain indefinitely. The locked contract explicitly acknowledges both JavaScript limits; tests prove neither can emit post-stop output or reopen state, so no high-severity lifecycle threat is left untreated.
</threat_model>
